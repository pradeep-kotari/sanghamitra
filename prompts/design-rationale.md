# Design rationale — Sanghamitra, second iteration

Written for Sreenivas Garu. Two pages, no jargon. This explains what changed on the
site and why, so when you look at it you should recognise your own organization.

---

## 1. The one decision everything else follows from

Your old site led with photographs. The site Pradeep built led with type — quiet
serif letters on warm paper, like a page from a book. In the meeting you asked for
photographs, tiles, and scrolling galleries again. Those two instincts pull in
opposite directions, so here is how I settled it, deliberately:

**The words keep their voice; the photographs carry the doors.**

The name, the mission, and the headings stay in the same book-like serif, because
that is what makes the site read as *yours* — a man who writes poetry and published
a magazine for twelve years. But the moment a visitor has to *choose where to go*,
a photograph does the work, not a word. Every area of the work is now a card with a
real photograph on top and the name beneath it. Below the main screen the scrolling
row of photographs is back, the way your old site had it. Nothing about the type
changed; what changed is that choosing is now done by looking, not reading.

## 2. The homepage, top to bottom

1. **The name is the largest thing on the page.** "Sanghamitra" in very large
   letters, with సంఘమిత్ర beneath it, aligned to the right edge — exactly as you
   placed it in the meeting. Hovering the name (here and on every page) shows
   *Friend of Society* and your motto: Knowledge, Entertainment and Progress —
   Vijnanamu, Vinodamu, Vikasamu.
2. **Your mission, verbatim, right under the name:** "Sanghamitra is an
   organization founded in 2003 based on values, love, sharing and caring.
   Education for progress. It's a platform to learn, share and progress."
3. **The seven areas of work as photograph cards.** Math Tutoring, Vedic
   Mathematics, Community Service, Knowledge Sharing, Works of Literature,
   Workshops, Events. Long names like "Community Service" wrap to two lines on
   purpose so all the cards line up — you asked for that. The grid does not care
   whether your final list has six, seven or eight cards; it reflows without any
   redesign. **Telugu is not its own card** — it lives inside Community Service,
   because the classes are SiliconAndhra Manabadi's.
4. **The scrolling photographs**, one row you swipe sideways: the 2026
   Medhavadhanam hall, both India Post photographs, the Harikatha and Kalyanam
   invitations, the YouTube channel, and your old magazine emblem.
5. **The next gathering**, as before, straight from your announcements.

The older arts and the magazine are not cards, but they are still one tap away —
from the line under the cards and from the footer, which I did not touch at all.

## 3. A category page, and the pattern the rest will follow

**Community Service** is built as the complete example — the hardest one, because
its name is longest and its photographs are the weakest. Every category page you
approve later will follow the same shape:

1. The name of the area, one quiet sentence under it.
2. Its sub-activities as simple cards (for Community Service: the drives; school
   fees paid **directly to the school** so the money cannot be misused; Telugu
   classes with SiliconAndhra Manabadi; community presentations).
3. A scrolling strip of photographs **of that activity**.
4. Buttons for what a visitor can do next.

The important part: the photographs on these pages are **not hard-coded**. When you
upload a photograph from your admin page and tag it with an activity, it appears on
that card and in that page's gallery by itself. Until a photograph exists, the card
shows a small honest note — "Photograph on its way" — never a broken frame. Your
`topic/year` folder habit maps onto this: upload by topic, and the site places them.

## 4. Events, split the way you described

One page, two halves. **Upcoming**: the invitation, a month calendar you can look
at (marked days have sittings; tap the day to jump to its invitation), and a button
that adds the sitting to a visitor's **Google Calendar** in one click — plus the
existing "Add to calendar" file for people on other calendars. **Past**: grouped by
year — 2026, and earlier years as they are added — newest year first.

## 5. What happens in Telugu

The తెలుగు button is on every page, top right. Tapping it switches the page and the
choice is remembered on the next visit. The dictionary starts almost empty **on
purpose**: nothing is machine-translated. Wherever a Telugu line is missing, the
English shows instead — the page never goes blank. Telugu text runs a fifth to a
third longer than English, so no box on the new pages has a fixed height; cards and
captions grow with the words. Only you can fill the Telugu in — the keys are ready
and waiting.

## 6. What I decided myself, stated plainly

1. **The magazine sits under Works of Literature** (your 21:27 reading), and stays
   reachable from the footer and the line under the cards. If you prefer Knowledge
   Sharing, one line moves.
2. **Past events are grouped by year**, the way you described them aloud.
3. **The language toggle switches the whole site**, falling back to English
   wherever Telugu is not yet written.
4. Two photographs are used as card pictures until better ones arrive: your old
   magazine emblem for Works of Literature, and the YouTube channel screenshot for
   Knowledge Sharing. Both are really yours; both are listed for replacement in
   `research/photo-shot-list.md`.
5. The phrase about the organization never appearing as "not for profit" is
   applied everywhere, including the saved site text it used to live in — it is a
   legal claim, and your own mission sentence says it better.
